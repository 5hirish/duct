# Educational Carousel — one idea per slide, built to be saved

**The format for teaching.** A carousel is the highest-engagement format on LinkedIn (document posts) and on Instagram by engagement per reach, because every swipe is a signal and a saved post comes back for weeks. This recipe optimises for the two numbers the feeds reward: swipe-through rate and saves.

---

## Philosophy

A carousel is a lesson with a cliff at the end of every slide. The reader should finish each slide knowing one thing and wanting the next. Nothing is explained twice, nothing needs the caption to make sense, and the last slide gives them a reason to keep it.

Text leads. Images support. A plain, high-contrast text card beats a decorative photo that hides the point.

---

## Slide Structure (6–10 slides)

Default **8**. Go to 10 only when the lesson genuinely has that many steps; stop at 6 for a tight three-point idea. Never pad.

| Slide | Role | What it does | Caption style |
|-------|------|--------------|---------------|
| 1 | Hook | The promise or the pain, in under 10 words. States the payoff of swiping. | `hook` |
| 2 | Stakes | Why this matters now — one line of context, one surprising fact or a reframing. Ends on a tension. | `cap-stroke` or `body-neutral` |
| 3 to N−2 | One idea each | One point per slide: a short headline (≤ 7 words) and one supporting line (≤ 20 words). Number them only when order matters. | `body-neutral`, `cap-pill` for a comparison |
| N−1 | Recap | The whole lesson on one slide, as a list the reader can screenshot. This is the save slide. | `body-neutral` |
| N | CTA | One ask, matched to the objective: save, follow, comment a word, or read the caption. Never three asks. | `cap-stroke` |

**Treat slide 2 as a second hook.** Instagram resurfaces a carousel from its second slide to people who scrolled past the first; LinkedIn previews two pages. Slide 2 has to work alone.

---

## Hook patterns that fit this format

- The number promise: "5 pricing mistakes that cost you the deal"
- The contrarian: "Stop writing a content calendar"
- The named enemy: "The 'quick sync' is killing your afternoons"
- The curiosity gap: "The one metric nobody puts on the dashboard"
- The before/after: "How we went from 0 to 40 signups a week"

Avoid: a question the reader can answer "no" to, a hook that names the brand, a hook that promises what the slides do not deliver.

---

## Copy rules

- One idea per slide. If a slide needs "and", split it.
- Headline ≤ 7 words, body ≤ 20 words, no more than 25 words on any slide.
- Concrete over abstract: a number, a name, an example beats "leverage" and "optimise".
- Parallel structure across the idea slides (all verbs, or all nouns, or all "X → Y").
- The recap slide repeats the idea headlines verbatim. It is a list, not a paragraph.
- No slide counters ("1/8"). They signal a list and give permission to leave.

---

## Visual rules

- **Default to text cards** (`kind: text`, `body-neutral`): a dark or brand-neutral card, headline large, supporting line smaller. Consistent across the carousel.
- A photo slide (`kind: photo`) only where a picture carries the point — a screenshot, a diagram, a before/after. Then the caption is `cap-stroke` or `cap-pill`.
- One accent colour across the whole carousel, used for the headline or a highlight, never both.
- Slides render 9:16. Keep the right 180px and bottom 300px clear of text (TikTok UI) and the copy centred, so a 4:5 crop for LinkedIn or Instagram loses nothing.
- No logos on slides 1 to N−1. A small handle on the recap slide is fine.

---

## Image prompt rules (when a slide has an image)

- Describe the single object or scene that makes the point. One subject, one light source, one background.
- Flat, editorial, uncluttered. "Minimal", "lots of negative space", "soft directional light".
- Leave the lower 40% of the frame darker or empty for the caption.
- No text, numbers or UI in the generated image. Words go in the caption layer.
- Avoid stock-photo tells: handshakes, laptops at 45°, people pointing at whiteboards.

---

## Caption (below the post)

The caption is the long version for people who want more and the place for keywords:

1. First line restates the hook in different words (it shows before "more").
2. Three to six lines expanding the idea slides, one per line.
3. The CTA from the last slide, once.
4. Three to five specific hashtags on Instagram and TikTok; none or one on LinkedIn.

---

## What makes this format fail

- Two ideas on one slide — the reader stops reading, not swiping.
- A hook that is a title ("Content strategy tips") instead of a promise.
- Slide 2 that only makes sense after slide 1.
- A recap slide missing — the save rate halves.
- Decorative photos behind every slide — the point disappears into the picture.
- More than 25 words on a slide; more than 10 slides.
- Three CTAs on the last slide.
- A brand logo on the hook.
- Padding a 5-point idea to 10 slides.

Monetizing Duct

Price the work Duct does, not the data it shows.

20 Aug 2026 Status pre-revenue, free beta Billing code today none Public promise grandfathered beta pricing
Superseded in part — Aug 2026

The credit-metered tier ladder in section 04 below is superseded by the-10x-question.html, which replaces it with flat, dollar-denominated pricing. Metered credits hedge against rising input costs; input costs are falling roughly 90% a year, so the hedge runs the wrong way.

Everything else here still stands — the value-metric analysis, the revenue lines beyond subscription, the funnel mechanics, the build list, and the sequencing. Read the 10x doc first for the strategic frame, then this one for the tactics.

01 · The read

You built something more expensive than your pricing docs assume

The strategy docs price Duct as a weekly brief — $299 Growth, $499 Pro, benchmarked against SEO dashboards. What actually shipped is an operator: it audits, plans, writes, generates images and video, publishes, and is growing the ability to apply changes in Google Ads and GA4 behind approval gates.

Those are two different businesses with two different comparison sets, and the comparison set is what sets the ceiling:

What you sellBuyer compares againstTheir anchorCeiling
Insight — briefs, audits, dashboardsSemrush, Ahrefs, Similarweb, "I'll just ask Claude"$99 – $249/moCompressing
Work — plans, content, publishing, applied fixesFreelancer, content marketer, agency retainer$2,000 – $8,000/moExpanding

The insight half is being commoditized in real time — every frontier model does competent SEO analysis now, and the platforms are shipping their own copilots. The work half is not, because it needs the boring compounding assets you already have: OAuth into GSC/GA4/Ads, a memory of the account, a publishing path, and an approval-gated change model.

The single highest-leverage decision: position Duct against labour, not against tools. Every packaging choice below follows from that. At $299 you are a cheaper Semrush; at $799–$2,500 you are a cheaper marketer — and the second is both easier to justify and less crowded.

02 · Value metric

Choose what the meter measures before you choose the number

Price level is easy to change later. The value metric is nearly impossible to change once customers are on it, so it's the decision worth spending real thought on. Six candidates, judged against Duct's actual shape — agentic, low-headcount, unbounded marginal cost:

MetricTracks valueExpandsMargin-safeLegible to buyerVerdict
Seats WeakWeakNoHigh Reject — an agent does the work, so a 3-person team buys the same value as a 30-person one, and per-seat taxes collaboration
Connected sources WeakSomeNoMedium Reject — punishes the integration depth that is your moat
Projects — site, brand, or client StrongStrongPartialHigh Primary axis already the account structure in your schema; the natural agency multiplier
Work credits — completed agent runs StrongStrongYesMedium Secondary axis the only thing that makes video and long agent sessions safe to sell
Published outputs — posts, reports StrongStrongPartialHigh Use as the human-readable label on credits — sell "40 posts", meter credits underneath
% of managed ad spend StrongStrongYesMedium Hold for phase 3 — needs the execution framework live and audited first

Recommendation: projects × included work credits, with credits always surfaced to the customer in the unit they think in ("28 posts and 4 audits left this month"), never as raw tokens. Tokens are your problem, not theirs.

03 · Options

Four packaging architectures

These are genuine alternatives, not a ladder — A and C are defensible choices if you weight the tradeoffs differently than I do.

Option A

Classic tiered SaaS

Three flat tiers, feature-gated, unlimited usage. $99 / $299 / $699. What the current docs implicitly assume.

ForFastest to ship, zero metering infra, easiest to sell and to quote
AgainstUnbounded COGS — one agency running 40 audits a day erases the margin on ten customers
AgainstYou can never include video or long agent sessions at any price
FitOnly viable with hard project caps and generation excluded entirely
Option B · recommended

Platform fee + work credits

Base subscription per workspace with included credits; extra projects and credit packs are the expansion path. Weight credits by real cost — a video post costs what a video post costs.

ForMargin-safe by construction; expansion revenue without a re-sell
ForLets you ship expensive features (video, deep audits) instead of refusing them
AgainstCredit anxiety suppresses the very usage that drives retention
AgainstNeeds a usage ledger and entitlement layer you have not built
Option C

Outcome / spend-linked

10–12% of managed ad spend, or a fee per applied change set. Priced the way agencies already price themselves.

ForHighest ceiling by far — $2k–$5k ACV without argument, because it's the budget line they already have
ForPerfectly aligned incentives; almost impossible to churn while it works
AgainstNeeds attribution you can defend and a track record you don't have yet
AgainstLong sales cycle, and revenue drops when their budget does
Option D · complement

BYO keys / local shell

The desktop app with keychain-stored provider keys is already built. Sell it as a deliberately cheap tier where the customer pays their own model bill and you charge only for the platform.

ForNear-zero COGS; opens a prosumer and consultant segment below your enterprise price floor
ForAnswers the security objection outright — data and keys never leave their machine
AgainstCannibalisation if priced too close to Growth; weaker usage telemetry
FitRuns alongside B, not instead of it

Pick B now, add D immediately, graduate to C for the Ads mode in 2027. B is the only architecture where your gross margin survives an enthusiastic customer, and it is the shape every comparable AI product has converged on for exactly that reason.

05 · Beyond subscription

Six revenue lines that don't need a pricing page

Several of these are shippable in days because the machinery already exists, and each one is also a willingness-to-pay probe you can run while the beta stays free.

1 · Paid deep audit — $149, one-off

Your lead-magnet audit already runs anonymously with a Turnstile gate and a 24-hour cache. Add a paid tier of the same flow: full depth, competitor comparison, a prioritised fix list, and a 30-day re-check. No login required, credit the $149 against the first subscription month. This is cash this quarter, and more importantly it tells you what a stranger will actually pay before you name a subscription price.

2 · White-label audit widget — $99–$299/mo

Agencies want a branded lead magnet on their own site. Sell them the embed. You get distribution into their audience, they get leads, and every report carries a "powered by Duct" line. Near-zero incremental build.

3 · Duct as an MCP server / API — metered

You already ship MCP servers. Expose audit and insights as callable tools priced per call. In 2026 the buyer is increasingly another agent, and being the tool that other people's agents reach for is a distribution channel with no CAC.

4 · Implementation and migration — $1,500–$2,500

One-time, hand-sold, unglamorous, high margin. Also the mechanism by which you learn what the product should do next.

5 · Benchmarks — add-on or content moat

Opt-in, anonymised, aggregated: "your organic CTR versus 400 comparable B2B SaaS sites." Nobody else in your position can produce this, it compounds with every customer, and it makes excellent free content even if you never charge for it. Requires explicit consent and a real aggregation threshold — treat that as a hard constraint, not a checkbox.

6 · Referral revenue — small but free

PostBridge, generation providers, hosting. Not a strategy, but it is a few hundred a month for an afternoon of work.

06 · Margin

The unit economics work you have to do first

You cannot price an agentic product without knowing what a job costs, and right now nothing in the backend records that. This is the gating work.

  • Instrument cost per completed job, by type. One ledger row per finished agent run: job type, input/output/cache tokens, third-party spend, wall time. Everything below depends on this table existing.
  • Price against p90 usage, not p50. Median users never break your margin; the top decile does. Model the tier against the heavy user and let the median be pleasantly profitable.
  • Bank the prompt-caching win explicitly. Caching is already working on the custom system prompt — measure the cache-read ratio per job type and treat improving it as a margin project with a number attached, not a nice-to-have.
  • Route models by step. Cheap extraction and classification to Haiku; synthesis and judgement to Opus. Then sell "priority models" as a Scale feature, which converts a cost decision into a pricing feature.
  • Never bundle video below Scale. Third-party generation is the one cost that can go non-linear on you. Always metered, always weighted.
  • Offer BYOK at every tier as a discount, not only on desktop. It converts your worst-margin customers into your best ones and defuses the security objection at the same time.
  • Degrade, don't fail. At the soft cap: warn at 80%, queue, or drop to a cheaper model. A run that dies halfway because credits ran out is a churn event, and it will be the thing they remember.

Target: ≥75% gross margin at p90 usage per tier. If a tier can't clear that, the credits are underweighted, not the price.

07 · Conversion

Turning the free funnel you already have into revenue

The lead-magnet audit and the planned free-tool roster are the top of this funnel. Two changes make them monetising rather than merely list-building:

  • Move the paywall from the diagnosis to the fix. Show every problem free — the score, the broken pages, the ranking losses. Gate doing something about it. This is the upsell shape your own execution test already validated, and it is the honest one: the diagnosis is a commodity, the fix is the product.
  • Gate on export and share, not on results. The moment someone wants to send the report to their boss is the moment they'll trade an account for it, and it selects for the buyer rather than the browser.
  • Every free tool needs one specific upgrade line — "connect Search Console and Duct watches this every week" — not a generic Start Free Trial.

Benchmarks to hold yourself to: free audit → account 12–18% · account → paid 4–8% · paid deep audit → subscription 25%+

One obligation to honour

The landing page FAQs promise, in writing, that beta is free with no card and that early users get grandfathered pricing. Keep that promise exactly — and use it. Announce a beta end date, grandfather the cohort at 50% for 12 months, and you convert an open-ended liability into the most credible urgency you will ever have.

08 · Build

What has to exist before you can charge anything

There is currently no Stripe, no plan model, no quota, no entitlement check anywhere in the backend or app. Roughly two focused weeks:

  • Data model — plans, subscriptions, entitlements, usage_ledger, hung off the existing project/membership tables
  • Stripe Billing — checkout, customer portal, webhooks for lifecycle, tax handled by Stripe Tax
  • Entitlement middleware — one FastAPI dependency (require_entitlement("content.video")) and a matching client-side gate, so features are gated in one place rather than scattered
  • Metering — a ledger write on every completed agent job, with the credit weight and the true cost side by side
  • Internal cost dashboard — margin per account per month; this is the instrument you'll steer pricing with for the next two years
  • Customer-facing usage view — in human units, with a soft-cap warning at 80%

The membership table already carries owner and collaborator. Agency plans will need a third role — a client-facing viewer who can read reports but not spend credits. Cheaper to add now than to retrofit.

10 · Risk

What would make this wrong

  • The insight half commoditizes faster than the work half matures. The most likely failure mode. Mitigation is to accelerate execution and account memory — the parts a general model cannot replicate because they need your OAuth, your history, and your approval gates.
  • Credit anxiety flattens usage. Real and well documented. Mitigate with generous inclusions, human-unit display, auto-topup, and graceful degradation — never a hard stop mid-run.
  • $299 turns out to be above the self-serve line and below the sales-assisted line. This is the classic dead zone. The pilot phase exists to find out; if it's real, split into $99 self-serve and $999 sales-assisted and abandon the middle.
  • Platform terms constrain automated changes. Google Ads and GA4 automation has rules. The approval-gated design already handles this — keep the human in the loop as a product principle, not just a safety feature, and it becomes a selling point.
  • Agency demand doesn't materialise. If the two pilot agencies churn, the ACV ceiling drops and the whole ladder should compress downward toward a $99–$299 self-serve product with much tighter COGS.
Grounded in the current repo: modes and connectors in the backend, the anonymous audit lead magnet, project/membership roles, the Tauri desktop shell with keychain-stored provider keys, and the $299/$499 CAC assumptions in the paid growth plan. No billing, plan, quota, or Stripe code exists yet — every number here is a starting position for argument, not a decision.

## 1. Strategic Foundation

### 1.0 What is cluster blogging — and why it works

Cluster blogging (also called the **pillar-cluster model**) is a content architecture where one broad, authoritative page (the **pillar**) covers a topic at a high level, and a set of focused **cluster posts** each go deep on a specific subtopic. Every cluster post links back to the pillar; the pillar links out to all cluster posts.

**Why it works:**

- **Topical authority** — Search engines rank sites that demonstrate depth on a subject, not just sites with one good post. A complete cluster signals expertise.
- **Internal PageRank distribution** — The linking structure concentrates authority on the pillar page and ensures cluster posts benefit from it in return.
- **Compounding returns** — Each new cluster post adds to the overall topical authority, so later posts rank faster than earlier ones.
- **Structured conversion path** — Every cluster post has a clear role: attract the reader, establish trust on their specific problem, then direct them to the pillar or the product page.

The alternative — publishing isolated posts on unrelated topics — produces no compounding effect. Posts stand alone, earn authority alone, and convert inconsistently. Cluster architecture turns a blog into a system.

### 1.1 Why Duct competes on depth, not volume

Duct is a new domain targeting an ICP — growth marketers and PMs — who consume content professionally. They can tell immediately whether a post is written by someone who has actually run an SEO program or by someone summarising what one looks like. Generic content will not convert them.

The implication: do not publish 5 posts per week. Publish 1–2 posts per week that are genuinely better than anything ranking for the target keyword. One post that ranks position 3 for a high-intent term is worth more than 20 posts that rank nowhere.

The goal in the first 90 days is not traffic volume. It is topical authority in two narrow categories:

- **Cross-tool SEO analytics for growth teams** (Organic Growth vertical)
- **Cross-tool product analytics synthesis for PMs** (Product Intelligence vertical)

Google rewards sites that own a topic cluster. The strategy is to build two complete clusters before expanding.

### 1.2 The two-cluster strategy

Two pillar pages. Two vertical clusters. Every post belongs to exactly one cluster. No orphan content.

```
Cluster 1: Organic Growth / SEO Intelligence
  → Pillar: /blog/seo-intelligence-guide
  → Converts to: /for-organic-growth

Cluster 2: Product Intelligence
  → Pillar: /blog/product-intelligence-guide
  → Converts to: /for-product-intelligence
```

The existing marketing pages (`/for-organic-growth`, `/for-product-intelligence`) are conversion pages, not editorial pages. They should not be the ranking targets for informational queries. The editorial pillar pages handle rankings; the product pages handle conversion. The pillar pages link to the product pages at the point where the reader's pain is highest.

### 1.3 The content-to-conversion thesis

The blog is not a brand awareness play. It is a bottom-of-funnel conversion engine dressed as education.

The reader journey: ICP searches for a specific, felt problem → lands on a cluster post that describes their situation precisely → recognises Duct's framing of the cross-tool synthesis problem → follows a contextual inline CTA to the beta page.

This works only if the inline CTA is placed at the exact moment the reader's pain is fully articulated — not at the end, not in a generic sidebar, but mid-post, right after the problem is named. Every post in the plan follows this convention.

## 5. Internal Linking Architecture

Internal linking is the primary PageRank distribution tool on a new domain. Do not treat it as optional.

### The rule

- Every cluster post links **up** to its pillar page (mandatory, placed naturally within the content)
- The pillar page links **out** to every cluster post (update the pillar each time a new cluster post publishes)
- Cluster posts within the same vertical cross-link to each other when topically adjacent (not forced)
- Every blog post links to the product page (`/for-organic-growth` or `/for-product-intelligence`) via an inline CTA mid-post and an end-of-post CTA

### The link map

```
/blog/seo-intelligence-guide  [Organic Growth Pillar]
```

### Anchor text conventions

| Link type | Anchor text style | Example |
|---|---|---|

## 8. Measurement Framework

### 8.1 What to track and where

**Google Search Console — weekly:**
- Impressions trend by page — is Google beginning to surface the content?
- CTR by page — below 3% on a non-branded informational query usually means a title/meta description problem, not a content problem
- Average position by page — anything ranked 8–20 is a "push" candidate (add internal links, minor content update)
- Query-level data — what are people actually searching when they find your pages? Queries you didn't target often reveal the next post to write

**GA4 — weekly:**
- Organic sessions by page
- Engagement rate by page — target >55% for blog content
- Blog → beta sign-up conversion rate — set up as a GA4 conversion event on form submissions on `/for-organic-growth` and `/for-product-intelligence`. **This is the single most important metric in the framework.**
- Time on page for pillar pages — target >3 minutes (indicates genuine reading)

**Ahrefs — bi-weekly:**
- Domain Rating trend (slow-moving — check every 2 weeks, not weekly)
- Keyword ranking positions for all target keywords
- New backlinks earned — which content earns links organically tells you which topics the audience values most
- Organic traffic estimate trend

### 8.2 Success benchmarks by stage

**30 days — Foundation**

The target at 30 days is instrumentation, not traffic. SEO indexing takes 4–8 weeks from publishing.

- Both pillar pages indexed (verify in GSC Coverage report)
- All published cluster posts indexed
- GA4 conversion events firing correctly for beta sign-up
- Zero organic traffic target — do not optimise for this yet

**60 days — Early traction**

- 500–1,500 total organic sessions/month
- At least 2 posts appearing in positions 10–30 for primary keywords
- 1–3 beta sign-ups attributable to blog content (requires GA4 conversion tracking)
- Pillar pages receiving impressions for target terms in GSC

**90 days — Cluster authority forming**

- 2,000–5,000 organic sessions/month
- At least 1 post ranking in top 10 for a primary keyword
- 5–10 beta sign-ups from blog (cumulative)
- 3–5 referring domains (even a handful of relevant links is strong at this stage)
- Topical authority signal: Google ranking Duct for keyword variations never directly targeted — this indicates the cluster structure is working

### 8.3 Intervention signals

| Signal | What it usually means | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Post indexed for 6+ weeks, zero impressions | Query too narrow or page being filtered | Check GSC Coverage. Reassess keyword targeting. |
| Post ranked 15–30 for 8+ weeks with no movement | Needs authority push | Add 2–3 internal links from higher-authority pages. Consider expanding post with a related section. |
| Post ranking well (top 10) but low CTR | Title/meta mismatch | Rewrite title and meta description. Use GSC query data to see what searchers expected. |
| Blog traffic growing but zero conversions | CTA placement or tracking broken | Check GA4 conversion event is firing. Test inline CTA vs end-of-post CTA. |

### 8.4 Monthly 15-minute retrospective

Run this once a month. 15 minutes maximum.

1. Which 3 posts had the most organic sessions? Why? (keyword win, distribution spike, or backlink?)
2. Which cluster posts are in positions 8–20 right now? These are "push" candidates.
3. What are the top 5 queries GSC is ranking for with no dedicated content? These are your next posts.
4. What is the blog → beta conversion rate this month? Below 0.5% means CTA work needed. Above 2% means double the publishing rate.

## 9. Technical SEO Baseline

The site is static HTML — there's not much to configure. But a few things matter.

**Sitemap update:** Every new blog post URL must be added to `/sitemap.xml` manually at the time of publishing. Add this to the publishing checklist. Use `changefreq: monthly` and `priority: 0.7` for blog posts.

**Canonical tags:** Verify that `/blog/post.html` renders the correct canonical URL for each post (should be the post's permalink, not the template URL). If it's currently rendering `post.html?slug=...` as the canonical, fix this before publishing new content.

**Article schema markup:** The existing product pages use `WebPage` schema. Blog posts should use `Article` schema (or `BlogPosting` subtype). Add this to the `/blog/post.html` template so it applies to all posts automatically. Minimum required fields: `headline`, `author`, `datePublished`, `dateModified`.

**Internal link minimum:** Every new post must link to at minimum 3 other pages:
1. The cluster pillar page
2. One other cluster post (when available)
3. The product conversion page (via inline CTA)

**Robots.txt:** Verify `/blog/` is not accidentally blocked. Current robots.txt appears minimal — confirm crawling is unrestricted.

**Page speed note:** Fonts load from Google Fonts. On slow connections this delays rendering. Not a ranking emergency but worth monitoring via Core Web Vitals in GSC.

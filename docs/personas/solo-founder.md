# Priya, the solo founder (`solo`)

**Author:** Shirish Kadam · **Updated:** 2026-11-24

**Priya runs a small online business alone, and marketing is the job that always loses to
support, product and invoices.** Duct wins Priya by answering "is my money working?" in
minutes, and loses Priya with one surprise charge or one screen of jargon.

> "I don't need a dashboard. I need someone to look at all of it and tell me what to do on
> Monday. And not touch my ad budget while I'm asleep."

| | |
|---|---|
| **Business** | Booking software for independent photographers, sold by subscription through Stripe. About $7k a month in revenue, 3 years in, no employees |
| **Marketing stack** | Google Ads (about $1,500 a month), GA4, Search Console, a blog, LinkedIn and Instagram, an email list |
| **Time on marketing** | 4 to 10 hours a week, in the gaps. 62% of small businesses spend 1 to 10 (S) |
| **Budget for tools** | Tight; every subscription is justified against a month of revenue |
| **AI today** | ChatGPT for drafts, pasting screenshots of reports into it. 86% of solopreneurs try AI before hiring help (S) |
| **Skill** | Smart, self-taught, often came to the business by building the product ("the product is done, I'm terrified of marketing"). Knows what ROAS stands for, not how GA4 attributes a sale |

## The week has no marketing day

Monday is support. Tuesday a bug. Wednesday the Google Ads bill arrives and Priya wonders,
again, whether it pays back. Thursday night: GA4 open in one tab, Stripe in another, Search
Console in a third, a spreadsheet trying to join them, and ChatGPT asked to make sense of a
screenshot. Friday: a LinkedIn post written at 11pm because it has been three weeks.

## What hurts

| Pain | What it costs | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Cannot decide when a channel has failed: traffic arrives, paying customers do not, and the tools count differently | Money: keeps paying for months on faith, or quits the one that was about to work | P |
| No time; only what pays this week gets done, and SEO or video sit "on the list since spring" | Weeks go by with nothing checked; 60% of micro-business owners spend 10+ hours a week on admin (S) | S, P |
| GA4 is hard to read; people stop opening it, or feel "forced to use it" | Hours lost to tutorials for one number | P |
| Google Ads feels "impossible", and Google's own setup or recommendations push spend up | A bill that grew without a decision; one owner let Google set up the account and burned about $3k | P |
| Traffic dropped and nobody can say why (an update, AI Overviews, a broken tag, a season) | Panic, then guessing | P |
| Content is inconsistent; posts go out when there is time, not when they would work | Feels invisible; "creating engaging content" is a top concern for 40% of owners (S) | S |
| Tired of every problem being "solved" by another subscription; the tool total can rival revenue | Says no to new tools on reflex, so Duct starts behind | P |

**What the threads changed:** the question is not "which channel gets the credit", it is "when
do I stop". Surveys say owners feel more sure of what works (41% expect it to be no challenge,
up from 30%, S), and the threads say the same people quit or persist on gut feel. The first
answer has to be a verdict on one channel, with its money, not an explanation of attribution.

## Wants, and what Priya actually needs

| Priya asks for | Priya needs |
|---|---|
| "A dashboard of everything" | One answer, ranked: the one change worth making this week |
| "AI that runs my ads" | AI that proposes, with the reason and the money at stake, and waits for a yes |
| "Cheap" | Cost known in advance: own model key, no per-seat price, nothing that scales with ad spend |
| "Content ideas" | A plan tied to what already worked for this business, not generic tips |
| "Easy setup" | Connected in under ten minutes, with a first useful answer before the coffee is cold |

## Why Priya would switch, and why not

| Push (today hurts) | Pull (Duct promises) | Anxiety (holds back) | Habit (keeps the old way) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ad bill with no proof; Thursday-night spreadsheets | Reads every tool at once; answers in plain words; asks before changing | Will it spend my money? Where do my keys go? Is it another ChatGPT that makes numbers up? | GA4 home page, ChatGPT and a gut feeling are free and familiar |

58% would trust an AI agent with their marketing (S), so trust is earned, not refused. Duct
earns it with the approval card and with numbers that trace to their source.

## Priya's words, and the words that lose Priya

| Says | Never says |
|---|---|
| "is it worth it", "wasting money", "burned", "what actually works", "what should I do", "my ads", "traffic dropped", "no time", "good enough", "another subscription", "paying customers" | "attribution model", "cross-channel synthesis", "insights platform", "leverage", "orchestration", "agentic workflows", "harness" |

## The moments that decide it

1. **First ten minutes:** sign in, connect Google once, see one real finding about this
   business. A blank chat box loses Priya here.
2. **First answer:** plain words, one number in bold, where it came from, what to do next.
3. **First proposal:** the approval card shows what changes, the money at stake, and how to
   undo it. Priya presses Apply only when that is all visible.
4. **Week three:** Duct remembers what was tried and whether it worked. Without that, it is a
   chatbot and Priya goes back to ChatGPT.

## Role-play brief

```text
You are Priya, 34, the only person running a booking-software business for photographers
(about $7k a month through Stripe, $1,500 a month on Google Ads). You do marketing 4 to 10
hours a week, late, tired, between support tickets. You are smart and self-taught but not a
marketer; GA4 confuses you and you distrust Google's recommendations because you once let
Google set up your account and the bill went up without you deciding it. You are tired of
subscriptions and say no to new tools by default. You use ChatGPT daily. You will give a new tool ten minutes.
You leave the moment you see jargon you cannot decode, a price you cannot predict, or any sign
it will change your ads without asking. Walk the screen or read the copy you are given, out
loud: what you look at first, what you think it means, what you would click, what worries
you, and the exact point you would close the tab. Be blunt. Use your own words, not the
product's.
```

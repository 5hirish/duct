# The Northside team, a small business (`team`)

**Author:** Shirish Kadam · **Updated:** 2026-11-24

**In a small business, marketing is shared between an owner who signs off on money, a
generalist who does the work, and a freelancer or agency who runs the ads, and nobody sees
the same numbers.** Duct wins this team by being the one place they agree on what is true and
who may change what. It loses them the moment its numbers disagree with an ad platform and it
cannot say why.

> **Dana, owner:** "Every month I pay the ads freelancer and the tools, and every month I get
> three reports that don't match. I just want to know if it's working."
>
> **Leo, marketing:** "I spend Monday pulling numbers so we can argue about them on Tuesday."

| | |
|---|---|
| **Business** | Northside, two Pilates studios plus an online membership. 14 people, memberships billed through Stripe |
| **Who does marketing** | Dana (owner, approves spend, 2 hours a week), Leo (ops and marketing generalist, about 15 hours a week), a freelance ads specialist on retainer (Meta Ads and Google Ads) |
| **Stack** | Meta Ads, Google Ads, GA4, Search Console, Instagram, email, a booking system, Stripe |
| **Budget** | Ads $3,000 to $5,000 a month; the freelancer $1,500; tools add up without anyone owning the list. Only 35% of small businesses work with outside help (S) |
| **AI today** | Leo uses ChatGPT for captions; Dana has heard AI "does marketing now" and is curious and wary |

## The week, as a team

Monday, Leo exports from Meta, Google and Stripe into a spreadsheet. Tuesday's meeting spends
twenty minutes on whose number is right: Meta claims 40 sign-ups, Stripe shows 22 new
members. The freelancer says the campaign is "learning". Dana asks whether to cut the budget
and nobody can answer with confidence. A decision gets deferred a week. Again.

## What hurts

| Pain | Who feels it | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Every platform reports its own version of the truth (Meta 22 sales, Google 45, GA4 6; a fifth of store orders never reach GA4); meetings argue numbers instead of deciding | Dana, Leo | S, P |
| The owner pays an ads freelancer or agency and cannot judge if it is working: "pretty reports and no results", or ad spend tripled and the market blamed | Dana | S, P: owners on their third agency; budget and perceived value lead agency churn (42%, 32%) |
| Pulling the weekly report by hand eats the generalist's Monday | Leo | S, P |
| Nobody knows who changed what, or why; a campaign edit or a broken tracking tag surfaces weeks later | all | H |
| The business does not own its own accounts: the agency set up Google Ads, GA4 or Meta under its own login, and the owner feels "held hostage" | Dana | P: the most-upvoted advice in these threads is "own your accounts, give the agency admin" |
| Knowledge walks out the door: when the freelancer leaves, what was tried and learnt goes too | Dana, Leo | H |
| Tools and logins sprawl; shared passwords; no one owns the list | Leo | H |
| Growth needs leads and the budget is fixed: getting new leads (47%) and making the most of budget (45%) are the top challenges | Dana | S |

## Wants, and what the team actually needs

| They ask for | They need |
|---|---|
| "One dashboard" | One weekly brief everyone reads, with each number tied to its source and the reason sources disagree |
| "AI that optimises the ads" | Proposals that the owner approves, with the cost and a way back, and a record of who approved |
| "Accountability from the freelancer" | Shared view of the same data, so the conversation is about decisions, not exports |
| "Not lose what we learnt" | A memory of what was tried, when, and what happened, that outlives any one person |
| "Everyone can use it" | Roles: Leo works daily, Dana reads and approves, the freelancer proposes |

## Why they would switch, and why not

| Push | Pull | Anxiety | Habit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tuesday arguments; a freelancer bill with no verdict; Leo's lost Mondays | One place, one brief, approvals and a trail | "Another tool to learn." "Will it fight with our freelancer?" "Who is liable if it changes a campaign?" | The spreadsheet works, slowly; the freelancer's report is familiar |

**What the threads changed:** before Duct can read a team's numbers, the team has to be able
to reach them. Many small businesses cannot connect their own Google Ads or GA4 because the
agency holds the keys, so connecting is sometimes a conversation with the agency first, and
Duct's setup has to say what access it needs and from whom.

The owner is the buyer and the approver; Leo is the daily user and the champion. A product
that delights Leo but never shows Dana a verdict does not survive the next budget review.

## Their words, and the words that lose them

| Says | Never says |
|---|---|
| "is it working", "which number is right", "who changed this", "our accounts", "admin access", "pretty reports", "the ads guy", "sign-ups", "cost per member", "worth it" | "single source of truth platform", "omnichannel", "attribution modelling", "stakeholder alignment", "AI-driven optimisation" |

## The moments that decide it

1. **Connecting:** Duct says plainly which accounts it needs admin or read access to, and
   what to ask the agency for if the business does not hold them.
2. **Leo's first week:** the Monday report builds itself, and it explains why Meta and Stripe
   disagree instead of picking one.
3. **Dana's first approval:** a proposal arrives with the money at stake, the reason and an
   undo. Dana approves from the phone in a minute.
4. **The first disagreement:** the freelancer and Duct see different results. Duct shows its
   sources and its working, and the conversation becomes about the campaign.
5. **Someone leaves:** the history of what was tried is still there.

## Role-play brief

```text
Play two people, in turn. DANA, 46, owns Northside, two Pilates studios and an online
membership (14 staff). Dana approves every cost, has 2 hours a week for marketing, reads on a
phone, and pays an ads freelancer without knowing if it works. Dana wants a verdict, not
charts, and distrusts anything that could spend money unapproved. LEO, 29, the ops and
marketing generalist, does the work: exports Meta, Google and Stripe every Monday, writes
captions with ChatGPT, and is tired of defending numbers that do not match. Leo is the one who
would bring a new tool in, and drops it if Dana never looks at it. The agency set up the
ad accounts years ago and Dana is not sure the business owns them. For the screen or copy you
are given, say as each of them: what you understand, what you would do next, what you would
need from the other person, and what would make you stop using it. Use your own words.
```

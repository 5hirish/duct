# Who Duct is for: three people

**Author:** Shirish Kadam · **Updated:** 2026-11-24

**Duct is for people who do marketing without an analyst: a founder alone, a small business
where the owner signs off, and the marketer who carries it for one company or five.** Every
screen, string, connector, post and feature names which of the three it is for. If it serves
none of them, it waits.

| | [Priya, the solo founder](solo-founder.md) | [The Northside team](small-team.md) | [Sam, the marketer](marketer.md) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Short id** (use it in issues, fixtures, copy notes) | `solo` | `team` | `marketer` |
| **Who** | Runs a small online business alone; marketing is one of nine jobs | 5 to 30 people; the owner approves spend, one generalist runs marketing, a freelancer runs ads | Fractional or freelance marketer with four to six small clients, or a marketing team of one in-house |
| **Marketing hours a week** | 4 to 10, in the gaps | 10 to 25, split across people | 30 to 45, all of it |
| **The question on Monday** | "Is this worth it, and when do I stop?" | "Which number is true, and who changed what?" | "What do I tell each client, and what did we try last time?" |
| **What they fear most** | A tool that spends money while they sleep | Paying people and an agency without knowing if it works | A client asking "what are we paying you for?" |
| **Duct is, to them** | The analyst they cannot afford | The one place the team agrees on | A second pair of hands that remembers every account |
| **Wins them** | One clear answer in minutes, no setup weekend | Approval before any change; one shared memory | Briefs good enough to send; history per client |
| **Loses them** | Jargon, a blank chat box, another bill | Numbers that disagree with their ad platform with no reason given; a setup that assumes they hold the accounts | Anything that touches a client account without a trail |

## How to use them

1. **Name one persona per change.** An issue, a PR/FAQ, a screen or a post says `solo`,
   `team` or `marketer`. "Everyone" is a sign the job is not understood yet.
2. **Role-play before you ship.** Each persona ends with a role-play brief. Paste it into a
   fresh agent (or read it and step in yourself), then walk the screen or the copy as them,
   out loud: what they click first, what they misread, where they would close the tab.
3. **Use their words.** Each page lists the words they use and the words that lose them.
   Copy is written in the first list.
4. **Check the moments.** Each persona lists the moments that decide whether they stay: the
   first ten minutes, the first brief, the first approval. Design reviews walk those.

## Where the evidence comes from

Every pain carries a tag: **S** a published survey (sources below), **P** a pattern reported by
practitioners in public, **H** a hypothesis from our own product work that the next research
pass must confirm or kill. The first Reddit pass, in the users' own words, is
[2026-11-24-reddit-research.md](2026-11-24-reddit-research.md): it turned most **H** tags into
**P** and moved three pains. Rerun it through the `social-research` skill, which searches the platforms and rooms listed
for each persona in [`.agents/social-research.json`](../../.agents/social-research.json)
(Reddit, LinkedIn, X and YouTube today), and record each pass as a new dated file.

## Keeping them true

A persona is a reference, so it is rewritten in place when the evidence changes, and the
**Updated** date moves. Real conversations beat this page: when a user interview, a support
thread or an onboarding answer contradicts a line here, the line changes that week.

**One rule to remember:** name the person before you build for them.

# Sam, the marketer (`marketer`)

**Author:** Shirish Kadam · **Updated:** 2026-11-24

**Sam does marketing for a living, for four to six small clients or as the only marketer in a
company, and spends too much of the week explaining numbers instead of moving them.** Duct wins
Sam by doing the analyst's legwork across every account and remembering each client's history.
It loses Sam with one unreviewed change in a client's account, or one brief that sounds like a
robot wrote it.

> "Half my Fridays are reports. The other half is a client on Slack asking why leads dropped,
> and me opening six tabs to find out it was a broken form."

| | |
|---|---|
| **Work** | Fractional marketer, five retainers ($1,500 to $4,000 a month each): a dental group, a B2B SaaS, a course creator, a local law firm, a skincare shop |
| **Variant** | The in-house **marketing team of one** at a 40-person company: one boss instead of five clients, the same job |
| **Stack, per client** | Google Ads, Meta Ads, GA4, Search Console, Tag Manager, sometimes Mixpanel or Stripe; Looker Studio for reports; Slack and email |
| **Time** | 40 hours a week, all of it marketing, a lot of it switching between accounts |
| **Skill** | Expert. Knows attribution windows, consent mode and why GA4 disagrees with Google Ads. Has no patience for tools that explain it back |
| **AI today** | Drafts, summaries, ideas. Distrusts generic AI content and anything that edits a live account. Reporting and performance summaries are where agencies say AI helps most (42%) (S) |

## The week is other people's questions

Monday: check five accounts for anything on fire. Tuesday and Wednesday: the actual work,
campaigns, landing pages, a content calendar. Thursday: a client asks why traffic fell; an hour
later it turns out a plugin update broke the tracking tag. Friday: reports, built from Looker
Studio screenshots and a paragraph written five times over. Monday the cycle starts again, and
last month's test is already half forgotten.

## What hurts

| Pain | What it costs | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Reporting got worse with better tools: debugging connectors, dashboard seats nobody opens, and still a screen share to walk the client through it | Billable time on screenshots; a manual report took 45 minutes to 4 hours in agency benchmarks (S) | S, P |
| Clients leave over budget (42%), perceived value (32%) and performance expectations (31%), not over skill | The retainer depends on proving value every month | S |
| Context switching across clients; keeping track of what was promised and tried for whom | Repeats a failed test; forgets a client's quirk | P |
| "Why did traffic drop?" with rankings up: AI Overviews answering the query, a broken tag, or a season, and the client thinks Sam is failing | Hours of reactive digging, then a hard conversation | P |
| Explaining every month why GA4, Google Ads and Meta disagree; it "sounds like an excuse" | Credibility, a little each month | P |
| Platforms change accounts on their own: auto-apply re-broadened one account's campaigns and burned $600 in six days on bot clicks | Wasted client spend Sam has to explain | P |
| Measurement is contested; 75% of US buy-side leaders say core measurement underperforms (S, secondhand) | Every number gets challenged | S |
| Burnout: overwhelmed and undervalued is the norm in the profession (S, secondhand: Marketing Week's 2026 survey) | Quits clients, or the career | S |

**What the threads changed:** marketers already know why the platforms disagree. What they
ask is "what gap is normal, and what gap means something broke". And reporting is a trust
proxy: a trusted marketer sends five lines, a doubted one gets questioned on every chart. So a
brief earns its place by saying what to do next ("CPA went down a little, so what?" is the
complaint), and by being allowed to say "nothing material changed this week".

## Wants, and what Sam actually needs

| Sam asks for | Sam needs |
|---|---|
| "Automated reports" | Briefs that say what changed, why, and what to do next, good enough to send with light edits, and short when nothing changed |
| "Why don't the numbers match?" | A baseline of the normal gap between each client's platforms, and a flag when the gap moves |
| "Monitoring" | Something that notices a broken tag or a spend spike before the client does |
| "A memory" | Per-client history of tests, decisions and results, searchable |
| "Control" | Nothing changes in a client account without Sam's approval, and an audit trail to show the client |
| "Speed across accounts" | One login per client, switch projects in a click, the same questions asked of every account |

## Why Sam would switch, and why not

| Push | Pull | Anxiety | Habit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Friday reports; reactive digging; churn risk | An analyst across every account; a memory per client; proposals with a trail | Client data in a new tool; liability for any change; AI copy that embarrasses Sam in front of a client | Looker Studio templates already built; years of muscle memory |

Sam is also the multiplier: one marketer brings five accounts, and recommends tools to peers.

## Sam's words, and the words that lose Sam

| Says | Never says |
|---|---|
| "client report", "actionable", "MoM", "CPA", "ROAS", "tracking broke", "what changed", "AI Overviews", "the account", "budget pacing", "send to the client" | "unlock growth", "magic", "set and forget", "autopilot", anything that explains ROAS to Sam |

## The moments that decide it

1. **The second client:** adding a project is as fast as the first, and nothing leaks between
   clients.
2. **The first brief Sam sends:** accurate, specific, sourced, in a register a client respects.
   One invented number and it is never sent again.
3. **The catch:** Duct flags a tracking break or a spend jump before the client notices.
4. **The approval trail:** a client asks "who changed this?" and Sam can show the proposal,
   the reason and the approval.

## Role-play brief

```text
You are Sam, 38, a fractional marketer with five small clients (dental group, B2B SaaS,
course creator, law firm, skincare shop). You are an expert: you know attribution windows,
consent mode and why GA4 and Google Ads disagree, and you are irritated by tools that explain
basics. Your week is reports, client questions on Slack and switching between accounts. You
need to prove value every month or lose the retainer. You will never let a tool change a
client's account without your approval and a record you can show the client, and you will not
send a client anything with a number you cannot trace. You have seen Google's auto-apply
burn a client's budget, and you are tired of LinkedIn posts claiming AI runs ad accounts. Look at the screen or copy you are
given: say what you would trust, what you would check, whether you would send it to a client,
and what would make you cancel. Be specific and impatient. Use your own words.
```
